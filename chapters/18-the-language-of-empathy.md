# Day 18 — The Language of Empathy

*November 19, 2026*

I had no idea empathy held so many caveats. I told myself that I knew what it was. I was far from understanding. I thought empathy was simply paying attention to someone and how they were acting to determine what they needed in the moment. Turns out it's more complex than that.

Empathy by definition is the ability to recognize, understand and share another person's emotions, thoughts or perspective, with four types that specify the different situations at hand. Affective empathy is the sensing or sharing someone's emotional state. Cognitive empathy is understanding another person's emotions without feeling them. Somatic empathy is feeling someone's emotions in your body, while compassionate empathy combines them all, providing emotional understanding with motivation to help someone suffering.

I wanted to understand the depth of this subject with clarity, so I took a few tests to see where I stood in emotional intelligence. First was the DERS test, Difficulties in Emotional Regulation Scale, 36 questions asked from different angles aimed at six areas of emotional experience: non-acceptance, goals, impulse, awareness, strategies, and clarity. The questions were not hard to answer and my results were below average difficulty. Yay. I had one part that needs to be worked on the most, and that is goals. I've actually been slipping in the last couple of days. I haven't stayed on my schedule properly, and I'm pretty sure it's from the lack of sleep. I will be better.

The second test was the RMET quiz, Reading the Mind Through the Eyes test, 36 photographs of people's eyes, and I had to select from four options describing what emotional state they were in. I've done well with a 31 out of 36 score. I was actually surprised to see this score. I guess the tough childhood was actually training me to be emotionally mature.

The third test was a little different. It was the Berkeley empathy quiz. I scored 85 out of 110, which again surprised me. I don't know why my confidence going into the test was low. Maybe it was just nervousness. Either way, I learned a lot of information. I'm truly beginning to understand myself. I've always cared for others. If they were upset, I felt moved to understand the situation. I found in doing so I became upset myself, because I was unable to regulate my own emotions. This caused me a lot of issues. I'm just now starting to become more grounded and present.

I'm excited to see what the new knowledge leads me into going forward. Emotional intelligence is imperative. Without this skill I am lost. Thank you, God, for allowing me to become enlightened. I pray with each day that passes I can learn more and become efficiently effective.
