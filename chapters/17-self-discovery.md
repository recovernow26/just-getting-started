# Day 17 — Self Discovery

*November 18, 2026*

Before entering into recovery, I thought that I knew who I was. After all I'd done to myself and others, I was surely a low life. A liar, a cheater, and a thief. A drug addict, to be exact — only to name a few. I was confident I could be nothing else.

I dreamed at times of becoming someone of importance. My fanatical way of thinking kept me distracted. I ignored the little actions I could take to get better, such as abstain from drugs and alcohol, stop lying, and quit betraying my wife. I chose instead to only dream while continuing toxic behaviors. I was who I was because of my actions.

I started to forgive the people that harmed me. "No more excuses" became my motto for a while and remains one of my principles to this day. Forgiving myself took longer, and I'm honestly still in the process. Setting goals, establishing boundaries, and letting go of the past has allowed me to become more aware of life's gifts. Success is really coming into focus as I aim my scope towards purpose and fulfillment. As a result, I'm building healthy bonds with people like never before. I'm sincerely amazed by the progress that I've seen in myself.

I miss my Amy to my core. She always wanted me to be the best version of myself. She would often say I had far more potential than where I was at. I guess she could see past my fear and doubt. Really, she was saying: stop doing the junk I chose to do and I would become a man.

The journey isn't over. Victories give way to new challenges; however, challenges offer opportunities for more victories. Focusing on recovery every day has healed my mind to the point that life is becoming enjoyable. I'm exceedingly joyful to wake up every morning to see what the day holds. I spent almost 40 years lost and depressed. I chose not to live that way any longer. I'm a work in progress; the rest is yet to be discovered.

Just for today, I will confidently face my fears. I will stand firm in my principles. I will not be moved.
